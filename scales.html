Size and carrying guide

Creature scale and egg weight

One internal scale controls each creature and its egg. See the size, weight, income and movement effects below.

A bell-shaped size roll

New scales use a bounded Gaussian (normal, or bell-shaped) distribution. Most rolls are near the quality band's midpoint. The standard deviation is one sixth of the band width; samples beyond the limits are clamped, so exact minimum and maximum rolls are rare but possible. The roll is server-owned and stays with that creature.

What scale changes

  • Pet height = base creature height times scale.
  • Pet kg = base creature kg times scale squared, with the base recalibrated from height times length. Pet length = base creature length times scale.
  • Egg kg = base creature kg times scale. Egg weight is linear, not cubic.
  • Base egg height blends creature height (50% influence) with the cube root of creature weight (50% influence). The reference is a 4-stud, 4-kg creature with a 1.8-stud egg. Every egg dimension then multiplies directly by scale.
  • Normal income = base Cash per second times scale times the existing quality income multiplier. Petting is a separate temporary 3x.

The old four-stud pet height minimum and additive quality height bonuses no longer apply. The quality band already changes size. A 10 kg base with scale 1.2 makes a 12 kg egg; all its base egg dimensions multiply by 1.2. Its pet mass follows the separate squared rule, becoming 14.4 kg.

How egg weight affects carrying

Egg-weight slowdown was halved on 27 October 2026. Carrying multiplies movement speed by 1 - 0.5 * (1 - max(0.55, 1 / (1 + eggKg * 0.06))). This halves the actual speed lost at every weight, including the heaviest eggs.

The maximum slowdown is now 22.5% instead of 45%, so you always keep at least 77.5% of your normal movement speed. At normal speed 16, a 4 kg egg gives about 14.45 speed instead of 12.90, and an egg at the maximum penalty gives 12.4 instead of 8.8. Running or swimming training raises the relevant normal speed before this penalty.

Eggs in a nest or inventory show their rolled weight. The tables show the entire possible band, not an equally likely range: most eggs are near the midpoint.

Saved scale and older creatures

Schema 21 saves CreatureScale, species, quality and identity/history. Known creature measurements, egg measurements and normal income are computed from the catalogue and scale. Runtime UI/model projections may hold computed fields; the profile writer removes duplicate measurement fields from durable records. Species weight records persist their scale and compute kg when read.

Older size rolls map deterministically into their quality band without rerolling. IDs, ownership, quality, hatch dates, positions and incubation deadlines remain. Sizes, weights and future earnings adopt the new formulas; previously earned Cash is retained. Historical archived records and unknown legacy species remain preserved. New rolls, rather than migration, use the bell-shaped distribution.
